Internal jugular line

One page, for the trolley. The reasoning is in the long version.

Adult, right internal jugular, real-time ultrasound. Tick down. The three red boxes are the ones that turn a routine line into a disaster, and none of them takes more than a few seconds.

Before you scrub

  • Indication still stands, and is not “to measure the CVP”
  • Consent — patient, person responsible, or section 37 emergency, and it is written down
  • Platelets and INR known; under 50 and elective, consider transfusing
  • Scan both sides undraped — patent, compressible, where is the carotid in this neck
  • Side chosen; avoid subclavian entirely if kidneys are failing
  • Monitoring on and visible; assistant present and staying
  • Everything open before you scrub; all lumens flushed
  • Position: head down 10–15°, head rotated as little as possible

Sterile set-up

  • Hat over all hair, mask, eye protection, sleeves up, bare hands and wrists
  • Surgical scrub, gown, gloves; assistant in hat and mask
  • Chlorhexidine in alcohol — allowed to dry
  • Full-body drape, puncture site only; sterile probe cover, gel inside and out

Doing it

  • Re-identify vein and artery; compress to be sure
  • Local anaesthetic, and wait for it
  • Puncture with the needle tip in view at all times
  • Wire passes with no resistance; stop if it does not
  • Wire in 15 cm, no further — watch for ectopy
  • Confirm venous. Twice. See the stop box
  • Nick with the blade away from the wire
  • Dilate held near the skin, skin and tissue only, never let go of the wire
  • Catheter to ~15 cm right, ~18–20 cm left
  • Wire out, and look at it
  • Aspirate and flush every lumen
  • Secure; chlorhexidine dressing over a clean, dry site
Stop 1 — before the dilator

Blood colour and the absence of pulsatility prove nothing. Two confirmations, at least one objective: see the wire in the vein on ultrasound in two planes, and/or transduce or run a manometry column. If you transduce, check the scale is arterial.

Stop 2 — if you have dilated an artery

Leave it in. Do not remove and press. Call vascular surgery or interventional radiology now. Removal and compression fails in the great majority and has killed people; leaving it in place while theatre is arranged does not.

Stop 3 — before anything is thrown away

Say the guidewire out loud and hold it up. Assistant confirms. Nothing goes in the bin until the wire is accounted for. This is an omission error that happens to careful people who were interrupted — being careful is not the control.

Afterwards

  • Tip to lower SVC or upper right atrium
  • Film if it was difficult, left-sided, or you are unsure — not routinely
  • A film cannot tell you the line is venous. Transduce
  • Check for pneumothorax if the patient changes
  • Document: indication, consent basis, site, supervision, ultrasound, passes, how venous position was confirmed, depth, wire accounted for
  • Ask tomorrow, and every day, whether it is still needed

Everything here is reasoned out, with the evidence and the numbers, in the long version. If you are learning this, read that once before you rely on this.
