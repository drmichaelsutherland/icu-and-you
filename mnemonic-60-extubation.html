Extubation at fifty-five

Two halves. FAILED is why the tube came out too soon. REPEAT is what has to happen before it comes out again.

F
Fluid — and a heart that only fails once the pressure comes offWeaning-induced pulmonary oedema is not a footnote. Across 283 spontaneous breathing trials it accounted for 59 per cent of all weaning failures. Positive pressure had been quietly unloading a left ventricle that could not manage alone, and the moment it comes off, preload and afterload both rise together. The three things that predict it are the three things this age band specialises in — chronic lung disease (odds ratio 8.7), structural heart disease (4.5) and obesity (3.6). The passive leg raise tells you whether enough fluid has come off: once it turns positive, the next trial usually works.
A
AirwayLaryngeal oedema and stridor, the cuff leak test nobody did, and vocal cords nobody has looked at. A long intubation, a large tube, a traumatic intubation and female sex all push the risk up. This is the one failure mode you can measure before you commit.
I
Inspiratory muscle weaknessDiaphragm dysfunction at the point of weaning runs anywhere from a quarter to two thirds, depending entirely on how hard you look: 29 per cent by ultrasound, 63 per cent by phrenic nerve stimulation in unselected patients. It is not the same thing as limb weakness and does not reliably track with it, so a patient with a respectable handgrip can still have a diaphragm that cannot do the work. Where it is present, ventilation runs roughly three times longer.
L
LoadObesity, the chest wall, intra-abdominal pressure, and basal atelectasis that never reopened after the last laparotomy. Capacity was never the only variable in the equation. The work was always going to exceed it, and nothing about the spontaneous breathing trial on a pressure support of five told you that.
E
EncephalopathyDelirium, sedation with a long tail in a fatty compartment, and a patient who cannot protect an airway they can no longer clear. A cough that will not shift secretions is an extubation failure that has simply not happened yet; it is waiting for a few hours to pass.
D
DepletionPhosphate, magnesium and potassium, each of which weakens the diaphragm directly and each of which is free to measure. And the feeding nobody restarted after the last three procedures, in a patient who arrived with less muscle than the chart implies.
THE PRICE OF GETTING IT WRONG
Roughly one ventilated patient in five is reintubated at some point and one in nine within 48 hours. Hospital mortality among them is 18 per cent against 3 per cent in those never reintubated, an adjusted odds ratio near 5.9. Some of that gap is the tube and some of it is simply that sicker patients are the ones who get reintubated — the association is not proof of cause, and the authors say so themselves. But it is large enough that the decision deserves more than a glance at a blood gas on the way past.

And then

R
Reintubate, and do not waitThe counter-intuitive one, and the most important thing on this page. In a randomised trial of non-invasive ventilation for established post-extubation respiratory failure, reintubation was identical in both arms at 48 per cent — but the machine pushed the median time from failure to intubation from about two and a half hours to twelve, and ICU mortality rose from 14 to 25 per cent. The trial was stopped early. Nothing was gained and the delay itself was the harm.
E
Electrolytes and energyPhosphate, magnesium and potassium corrected, and then rechecked rather than assumed. Feeding restarted, and protected from the next fasting order somebody writes at midnight for a scan that happens at four.
P
Pressure — before, and not afterThe same machine used prophylactically does the opposite of what it does in rescue. In high-risk patients extubated after more than 24 hours — over 65, or carrying cardiac or respiratory disease, obesity hypoventilation included — non-invasive ventilation alternating with high-flow oxygen cut reintubation at day seven from 18.2 to 11.8 per cent. It goes on at extubation, while they still look well, not when they begin to tire.
E
Erect and exercisingSat up, mobilised, physiotherapy, cough assist, and a bronchoscopy if a lobe has gone down and stayed down. Load you can rarely change in an afternoon. Capacity you sometimes can.
A
Airway provenIf the cuff leak test fails, steroids before the next attempt: in selected patients the reintubation risk falls by about two thirds, with a number needed to treat of five to prevent post-extubation respiratory failure. In unselected patients they do nothing at all. And the test has a poor positive predictive value for stridor, so the drug that works does so through a filter that is itself mediocre — which is an argument for using the test, not for trusting it.
T
Tracheostomy, or a dateDecide, and write the decision down. The third attempt that happens by drift, at four in the afternoon, because the bed is needed and the consultant has gone, is the one that goes wrong.
THE SAME MACHINE, TWICE
Non-invasive ventilation put on before they fail: reintubation 11.8 against 18.2 per cent. Non-invasive ventilation put on after they fail: mortality 25 against 14 per cent, and a trial stopped early. One intervention, opposite signs, and the only variable that changed is whether somebody waited to be sure.
And the rest —
Post-extubation dysphagia, which is common after a long tube and is almost never looked for. The unplanned self-extubation, which is a different animal and succeeds more often than anyone expects. Obstructive sleep apnoea that should have had CPAP from the first night rather than the third attempt. Hypothyroidism. Myasthenia unmasked by an aminoglycoside. And the patient for whom none of this applies, because the decision not to reintubate was made properly and in advance, which is the one version of this page that ends well.

Challenge for you

What did you find the second time?

Everyone has extubated somebody twice. The useful half of the story is almost never the first attempt — it is what turned up between them. The phosphate of 0.3. The echocardiogram nobody had done. The partner who said, when finally asked, that he stops breathing all night.

So: an extubation that failed, and what you found before the next one. And if it is the uncomfortable version — the one where the second attempt failed too and the tracheostomy should have been three days earlier — that is the more useful letter, and it can go up without your name on it.
