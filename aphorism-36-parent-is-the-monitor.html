This week's aphorism
“In an infant, the parent is the monitor.”

Preamble

An infant compensates beautifully, and then stops. Vasoconstriction and tachycardia hold a normal blood pressure long past the point at which an adult would have declared themselves, so the chart at the end of the bed stays reassuring while the reserve quietly runs out. By the time the numbers move, you have lost the part of the illness you could most usefully have influenced.

Meanwhile someone in the room has been watching this child continuously for months. They know how she feeds, how she holds herself, what her cry means, how long she usually sleeps and what her face does when she is about to be sick. They are not describing observations; they are describing a change from a baseline that no instrument in the department has access to. That is not anxiety. It is data collected by the only person present who has the comparison.

And it is the earliest data available. Reviews of missed deterioration in children keep arriving at the same finding: the parent said something before the observations changed, and it was recorded as reassurance-seeking rather than as a sign.

Do you agree?  Yes / No / Maybe

Yes

The parent detects change before any chart does, because they hold the baseline. Treating “she is not herself” as an observation — charting it, escalating on it — catches deterioration at the only stage where it is cheap to catch.

No

Parental concern is real but wildly non-specific, and it is not evenly distributed: the anxious present early and often, the exhausted and the unsupported present late. Elevating it to a monitor risks both over-investigation and, worse, a false floor under the quiet family who never say anything at all.

Maybe

It depends what you mean by monitor. As an alerting signal, plainly yes. As a measurement, no — it has no threshold, no units and no calibration, and a first-time parent and a fourth-time parent are describing different instruments entirely.

Optional reflection

When did you last chart what a parent told you, in their own words, as an observation?
