The Brain Is a Person Too

The doppelgänger of lifelong learning is lifelong loafing, and it has more evasive strategies than a cloaked Klingon battlecruiser

As I sit in the conference room, I look around at the delegates.

Some are gazing intently at the speaker, and others are gazing intensely at their own computers, tablets or phone screens, or their own navels. Some are checking emails, some are checking sports results, and some are just checking what time lunch starts.

Some of the speakers are dynamic, and that is just dynamite. Engagement is easy and contagious. Not many speakers are like that. Others read out what is on the slides, and some of them read it badly. There is a hearty round of applause at the end of each speech, especially the last one before lunch.

The Japanese do a lot of interesting things with seafood. I like that. There are a lot of waiters and waitresses to look after us. I really like the way that everything seems to involve a little bow, and I bow back whenever it seems appropriate. They seem to appreciate that. I wonder what the bow really means. G’day? Have a good one?

I had rehearsed my presentation quite a bit the day before, so I was OK this time. A delegate comes up to me after the presentation and says he enjoyed my conversational style. I thank him. I hadn’t exactly been aiming for conversational, but I thought it was nice to be described as having a “style”.

“You definitely have your own style,” says ma chérie, adding that it is “very Anglo”. I think that is French for “you have no style”.

* * *

The Osaka conference, along with Berlin last month and Bangkok earlier in the year, is all part of my “lifelong learning” drive. I really like the idea of learning something new every day. When I am in form, this is a really thrilling type of activity. I learn, learn, learn, and I wake up every day thirsty for something new to learn. It feels like learning is a necessity, like breathing, eating, smiling and sleeping.

But I am not always in form. The evil twin, the doppelgänger of lifelong learning, is lifelong loafing, and that kicks in from time to time, too. Not that often with me, though. I start thinking about what loafing really entails, and then decide to look into it more closely. And now with AI, the possibilities for looking into something, everything, or nothing at all are endless, unbounded and infinite, everywhere, all at the same time.

All of the colleges and universities expound on policies of lifelong learning. I think it is paradoxical. If you are a lifelong learner, you don’t need to be told. And if you are a lifelong loafer, you will be very skilled at avoiding any attempt to push you onto the lifelong-learner path. You will be unavailable, unable and uncontactable. You will be available only when not contactable, and contactable only when unavailable. You will have more evasive strategies than a cloaked Klingon battlecruiser. Any learning that happens will be via the rather unreliable method of osmosis.

* * *

But don’t be like that. The brain is a person too, and needs to eat and drink. And imagine and create, and question and speculate.

Children have that unquenchable curiosity, and whatever remains of the child in all of us has it too. If you can regularly unlock that lifelong-learner box inside you, the colour in your world suddenly returns. People notice. You notice.

Live long and prosper.
