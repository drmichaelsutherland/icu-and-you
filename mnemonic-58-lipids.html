ICU and You · Mnemonic Series · Number 58

Infarct in the young

Six things, and the word is the answer: LIPIDS — etc.

L
Lipids — still two thirds of themAtherosclerosis is the single commonest cause at every age, and being young does not exempt anyone: roughly two thirds of infarcts under sixty-five are atherothrombotic. Familial hypercholesterolaemia accounted for 9% of infarcts under fifty, and 43% of those patients were not on a statin beforehand. And the modifiable one that dominates this age group is tobacco — in pooled Australian registry data, half of all acute coronary syndrome patients aged 20 to 54 were current smokers, against a quarter of the 55 to 74s.
I
InflammationKawasaki disease grown up, with aneurysms that were never followed; Takayasu arteritis; lupus and the antiphospholipid syndrome. Systemic inflammatory disease turns up in only about 2.5% of young infarcts, so this letter earns its place by teaching value rather than by frequency — but antecedent Kawasaki has been found in around 5% of under-forties investigated for ischaemia, which is a reason to ask about a childhood illness nobody has mentioned since primary school.
P
Pregnancy — and SCAD, which is mostly not pregnancySpontaneous coronary artery dissection causes about 43% of infarcts in pregnancy, and the peripartum cases are the severe ones — more than half of them inside the first week after delivery. But only ten to twenty per cent of SCAD is pregnancy-related at all. It is the leading non-atherosclerotic cause of infarct in young women generally: 11% of infarcts in women under sixty-five, and over half of those cases were initially misclassified. Management differs too — conservative in the great majority, with stenting reserved for ongoing ischaemia.
I
Idiopathic — and it depends how hard you lookedAbout 3% of infarcts under sixty-five end with no mechanism found. But that number swings wildly with effort: in registries without systematic imaging, around three quarters of infarcts with unobstructed coronaries have no cause identified, while putting the same patients through optical coherence tomography and cardiac MRI brings it down to about a fifth. The gap between those two figures is not biology. It is how much workup the patient got. Much of what the imaging finds instead is takotsubo — mostly a diagnosis of older women, though one in nine is fifty or under, and in intensive care it has a younger face after subarachnoid haemorrhage.
D
Drugs and radiationThe ones you find by asking, not by scanning. Cocaine and the amphetamines, by spasm arriving at the same moment as a rise in demand — the risk is highest in the first hour. Anabolic steroids in a young man who will not volunteer them. The combined pill with cigarettes, though that interaction belongs mostly to the over-thirty-fives. And mediastinal radiotherapy, which accelerates coronary disease by decades: median latency eighteen years from a median treatment age of twenty-seven, so they are irradiated in their twenties and infarcting in their forties.
S
Supply and demand — and StructuralSupply and demand is the second largest category in the best data — 23% of infarcts under sixty-five, and 34% in women — and it is the one you will actually meet on the unit: sepsis, tachyarrhythmia, profound anaemia, hypoxia, and the exertional presentation in somebody young and fit. Structural covers anomalous coronary origin and myocardial bridging, both of which are common findings and uncommon causes — a third of adults have a bridge and almost none infarct from it. Aortic stenosis belongs here too, bicuspid in this age group. And so does aortic dissection extending into a coronary ostium, which presents as an inferior infarct and is the one you must not thrombolyse.
